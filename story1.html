Nyoklnom's Story

Chapter 1

Nyoklnom was an ordinary young man living in a world where everyone seemed to know exactly what they wanted to become. Some dreamed of becoming doctors, others wanted to be successful business owners, and many simply wished to live comfortable lives without worrying about tomorrow.

But Nyoklnom was different.

He had a thousand dreams inside his head, yet he struggled to explain even one of them to other people. Sometimes, he imagined himself becoming successful enough to change his family's life. Other times, he dreamed of traveling to places he had only seen in pictures, meeting people who would change his perspective, and experiencing adventures that would one day become stories worth telling.

Unfortunately, dreams did not pay the bills, and imagination alone could not guarantee a better future.

Every morning, Nyoklnom woke up to the same routine. He would check his phone, look at the time, and remind himself that another day had begun. He watched other people celebrate their achievements online, and although he was happy for them, a quiet question always returned to his mind.

When will it be my turn?

He never said those words aloud. He was afraid that if he shared his dreams, someone would laugh at them. So he kept everything to himself, pretending that he had everything figured out when, in reality, he was still searching for his place in the world.

One rainy evening, while walking home, Nyoklnom stopped beneath the roof of an old bookstore. The rain poured heavily, forcing him to wait until it weakened. As he looked through the dusty glass window, something caught his attention.

An old book sat alone on a wooden shelf.

Its cover was black, its edges were worn, and its title was written in faded silver letters:

THE LIFE YOU HAVE NOT LIVED.

Curiosity got the better of him. He entered the bookstore and asked the elderly owner about it.

The man smiled strangely before handing him the book.

"Be careful with the stories you read," the old man said. "Some stories have a way of changing the person who reads them."

Nyoklnom laughed politely, assuming it was simply something an old bookstore owner would say to make a book sound interesting. He paid for it, placed it inside his bag, and continued home.

That night, he opened the book.

The first page contained only one sentence.

"The future belongs to those who are willing to become strangers to the person they used to be."

Nyoklnom read the sentence several times. For reasons he could not explain, the words felt strangely personal.

He turned the page, expecting a story.

Instead, he found a handwritten question.

"If you could change one thing about your life, what would you change first?"

He stared at the question for a long time.

Then, for the first time in months, Nyoklnom picked up a pen and began writing.

He wrote about his fears, his ambitions, his regrets, and all the things he wanted to accomplish but had been too afraid to attempt. He wrote until his hand hurt and his eyes grew heavy.

When he finally closed the book, something inside him felt lighter.

He still had no idea what his future would look like.

But tomorrow, he decided, he would do one thing differently.

And sometimes, one small decision is where every great story begins.
